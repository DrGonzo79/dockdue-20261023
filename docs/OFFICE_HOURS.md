# Office Hours — DockDue

**Mode:** Startup, pre-product. **Position:** real cash pain, but the report proves the category—not this offer. The founder must validate access and trust before building integrations.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** $3.6B estimated unrecovered expense, repeated carrier complaints, and existing paid tools establish behavior and spend. No carrier has authorized DockDue, paid it, or relied on it. Verdict: promising, unvalidated.
2. **Painful status quo:** a dispatcher reconstructs dwell from ELD/GPS, hunts BOL and rate con, calculates broker-specific terms, sends paperwork, and chases at least three times. The claim is often abandoned because $150–$300 is smaller than the interruption cost.
3. **Desperate specific user:** the owner-operator or dispatcher at a 5–30 truck refrigerated/spot-market carrier with thin back-office staffing, high detention exposure, and cash pressure. They fear lost cash and broker conflict more than they desire a dashboard.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** a manual 90-day audit returning a claim ledger and three broker-ready evidence packets. Charge nothing for the audit; ask for 20% only on recovered back claims. Do not sell the always-on $99/truck agent yet.
5. **Observation plan:** sit beside five dispatchers as they process one recent unpaid claim without coaching. Measure time-to-evidence, missing artifacts, policy ambiguity, abandonment point, broker response, and willingness to authorize a follow-up. Surveys and demos do not count.
6. **Future-fit:** if ELD/TMS APIs and freight agents normalize, calculation commoditizes. A permissioned recovery history, broker-policy corpus, evidence quality, partner distribution, and demonstrated recovery rate become more valuable. Without those, incumbents absorb the feature.

## Premise challenge

- The job is **recover cash**, not track detention. A prettier timer is the wrong product.
- The status quo wins when claim value is lower than the hassle. The product must reduce follow-up labor, not only draft an invoice.
- The prototype cannot prove recovery. It can falsify whether the workflow and missing-evidence feedback are understandable.

## Approaches considered

| Approach | Effort | Risk | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Manual audit + claim packets | S | Low | **Choose first.** Fastest trust and demand test; humans handle policy ambiguity. |
| Deterministic claim desk + manual sending | M | Medium | Prototype now; suitable after five observed workflows. |
| Fully integrated autonomous recovery agent | XL | High | Defer. API, authorization, compliance, and broker-relationship risk arrive before demand proof. |

## Assignment

Recruit five refrigerated or spot-market fleets through one factoring/dispatch partner. Observe one live claim each and offer to recover one aged claim on 20% contingency. Success is three data-authorizations and one accepted contingency agreement—not compliments or waitlist signups.
